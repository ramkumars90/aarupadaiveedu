Thiruparankundram திருப்பரங்குன்றம்

Also spelt Tirupparankunram and Thirupparankundram. Murugan here is worshipped as Subramaniya Swamy.

A cave temple cut into a granite hill on the edge of Madurai. This is the first abode, where Murugan married Deivanai after his victory.

The story of this abode

After defeating Soorapadman, Murugan came to this hill. In gratitude, Indra, king of the devas, gave him his daughter Deivanai in marriage. The wedding is the heart of this temple, and Panguni Uthiram, its anniversary, is the biggest festival here.

The sanctum is carved directly out of the rock. The Pandya kings cut the cave shrines around the 8th century, and the Madurai Nayaks later added the pillared halls and gopuram you walk through today.

Because the sanctum is part of the living rock, the main deity is not bathed. Only a fragrant paste called punugu is applied. Abhishekam is offered to Murugan's Vel instead.

What to look for

  • Five shrines cut into one rock face: Murugan, Durga, Vinayaka, Shiva and Vishnu, side by side.
  • Life-sized sculptures in the Nayak-era mandapams.
  • Murugan seated with Deivanai, in the pose of the wedding.
  • The Kasi Viswanathar shrine on top of the hill, for those who want a climb.

Rituals explained

Wedding vows

Couples come here to pray for a good marriage, and many weddings are held in the temple's halls on auspicious days.

Hall bookings at the temple office, with ID proof and photos.

Abhishekam to the Vel

A rite special to this temple. Since the rock sanctum cannot be bathed, devotees offer milk abhishekam to Murugan's spear.

Ticket at the counter.

Mottai (head-shaving)

Hair offering, often for a child's first tonsure, at the temple's tonsure hall.

Free at HR&CE temples. Insist on the official receipt.

Archanai

Chanting of the Lord's names with your name and birth star. Available in Tamil.

Small ticket fee at the counter.

Facilities and accessibility

Easy for elderly visitors

The temple sits at the foot of the hill, so there is no real climb. Inside, a few flights of steps lead up through the halls to the cave sanctum.

StepsA few flights inside the temple. No hill climb.
Hill-top shrineThe Kasi Viswanathar shrine on the summit is a steep, optional climb.
ParkingNear the temple on the car streets. Festival days get very crowded.
Combine withMeenakshi Amman temple in Madurai and Pazhamudircholai the same day.

Where to stay and eat

Stay

Most pilgrims stay in Madurai, 8 km away, which has hotels at every budget. The temple has marriage halls but few rooms for pilgrims.

Food

Madurai is famous for its vegetarian 'mess' meals and tiffin. Small vegetarian eateries line the streets around the temple.

Before you go

  • Go early on a weekday. Festival and wedding days fill the halls.
  • Dress traditionally. Cover shoulders and knees.
  • Leave footwear at the stand outside.
  • Pair this with Meenakshi temple and Pazhamudircholai for a full Madurai day.

Our tried-and-tested picks

From our own visit, October 2025

Stay

Madurai Residency, Madurai. Very close to the Periyar bus stand, and you can walk to Meenakshi Amman temple. A good base for this abode and Pazhamudircholai.

Getting there

We took the town bus from the Periyar bus stand, well under ₹20 per person. Coming back, we took an auto to reach the hotel quicker.

Eat

Sree Sabarees, Madurai. A very busy, typical Madurai-style vegetarian hotel.

How to reach

Directions in Google Maps

By train

Madurai Junction is 8 km away. Thiruparankundram also has its own small station, one stop south of Madurai on the line to Tirunelveli.

By air

Madurai airport is close by, about 15 minutes by car.

By road

Frequent town buses run from Madurai's Periyar bus stand. An auto or taxi from the city takes about 20–30 minutes.

Festivals at Thiruparankundram

Panguni UthiramPanguni (Mar–Apr). The divine wedding of Murugan and Deivanai. The biggest festival here.
Kanda SashtiAippasi (Oct–Nov). Six days ending with Soorasamharam.
Vaikasi VisakamVaikasi (May–Jun). Murugan's birth star.
Karthigai DeepamKarthigai (Nov–Dec). Lamps across the temple.

Nearby

  • Meenakshi Amman Temple, about 8 km, in the heart of Madurai.
  • Pazhamudircholai, the sixth abode, about 30 km north.
  • Alagar Kovil, the Vishnu temple at the foot of Pazhamudircholai.
