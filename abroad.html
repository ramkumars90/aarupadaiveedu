For pilgrims travelling from outside India

Visiting the six abodes from abroad வெளிநாட்டு பக்தர்களுக்கு

Murugan devotees come to the Arupadai Veedu from Malaysia, Singapore, Sri Lanka, the Gulf and far beyond. This page brings together what is different when you travel from abroad: entry customs, which airport to fly into, visas, money and temple etiquette.

Visitors of other faiths

Everyone is welcome to visit the six abodes, see the gopurams and soak in the atmosphere. By long-standing custom, Tamil Nadu temples set aside the inner area beyond the flagstaff (kodimaram) for worship. Visitors who are not Hindu are welcome as far as the flagstaff.

If you have faith in Murugan and would like darshan, you can go further. Visit the temple office (aluvalagam) near the entrance and sign a short form. It says you have faith in the deity and will respect the temple's customs, and the staff will guide you from there. Many devotees from other faiths do this.

Out of respect, please leave symbols of other faiths outside, as you would your footwear.

This follows Tamil Nadu law, as confirmed by the Madras High Court in 2024 and again in 2026. References: D. Senthilkumar v. Government of Tamil Nadu, W.P.(MD) 18485 of 2023, judgment of 30 Jan 2024 · Thangam Venkatesh v. State of Tamil Nadu, WP(MD) 30452 of 2026, judgment of 23 Oct 2026. Checked 23 Nov 2026.

Which airport to fly into

You don't have to go through Chennai. Tiruchi (Trichy) and Madurai have direct international flights and are much closer to most of the abodes.

Visa

Most visitors need a visa to enter India. Many nationalities can apply online for an e-Visa. Apply only on the Government of India's official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in. Other sites that look official charge extra fees.

Rules, fees and eligible countries change, so check the official site before you book. Overseas Citizens of India (OCI cardholders) do not need a visa.

Money and payments

Carry some cash

Archanai tickets, footwear stands, flowers, autos and small eateries often take only cash or UPI, India's phone payment system. Keep small notes (₹10 to ₹500) with you. ATMs are easy to find in every temple town.

Paying by phone (UPI)

Regular UPI apps need an Indian bank account. Visitors can use a prepaid tourist wallet instead, such as UPI One World. You sign up at counters at some major airports, or through an app, with your passport and visa. These wallets pay shops and counters, but usually not individuals.

Darshan tickets

Paid darshan tickets can be booked online on the HR&CE portal. If your foreign card doesn't work there, every abode sells tickets at counters near the entrance, in cash.

Temple etiquette for first-time visitors

Nobody expects visitors to know everything. These few things go a long way.

Before you go in

  • Leave footwear at the stand outside. Socks off too.
  • Dress modestly, covering shoulders and knees. A veshti or dhoti for men and a saree or churidar for women is the safe choice.
  • At some shrines men remove their shirts. Follow what others do.

Inside

  • Walk around the shrines clockwise, keeping the deity on your right.
  • Don't point your feet towards the deity when you sit.
  • Photography is not allowed inside. At several abodes phones must be left at the cloakroom.
  • Queue patiently. A quiet "Muruga" or "Om Saravana Bhava" is always welcome.

Prasadam and vibhuti

  • Receive prasadam and vibhuti (sacred ash) with your right hand, or both hands.
  • Apply vibhuti on your forehead, or keep it in a paper to take home.
  • An archanai is a short prayer the priest offers in your name. Buy a small ticket at the counter.
