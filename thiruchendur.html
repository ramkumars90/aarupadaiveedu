Thiruchendur திருச்செந்தூர்

Also known as Thiruchiralaivai (திருச்சீரலைவாய்), its name in Thirumurugatrupadai, and Thiru Jayanthipuram. Also spelt Tiruchendur. Murugan here is worshipped as Senthil Andavar.

Arulmigu Subramaniya Swamy Temple stands on the shore of the Gulf of Mannar. It is the only one of the six abodes by the sea, and the place where Murugan defeated Soorapadman.

The story of this abode

The asura Soorapadman held a boon that no ordinary being could kill him, and he tormented the devas. Murugan led his army (his padai) south and camped here. On the sixth day of battle, on these sands, he struck Soorapadman with his Vel. The asura's two halves became the peacock Murugan rides and the rooster on his flag.

That victory is why Kanda Sashti matters most here. Devotees fast for six days, and on the sixth evening the Soorasamharam is re-enacted on the beach before lakhs of people. The divine wedding, Thirukalyanam, follows the next day.

Arunagirinathar sang many verses of Tiruppugazh in praise of the Lord at Thiruchendur, which is also called Thiru Jayanthipuram, the city of victory.

What to look for

  • The tall gopuram facing the sea, visible from far along the coast.
  • The sanctum sits below ground level. Unlike at the hill abodes, you go down to meet the Lord.
  • Shanmuga Vilasa Mandapam, the pillared hall at the entrance.
  • Nazhi Kinaru, a small freshwater well right beside the salt sea.
  • Valli Guhai, a cave shrine to Valli on the shore nearby.

Rituals explained

Many pilgrims come to fulfil a vow (prarthanai). These are the most common ones here.

Mottai (head-shaving)

Offering your hair is one of the oldest vows to Murugan, often made for a child's first tonsure or in thanks for a prayer answered. Shaving is done at the temple's tonsure hall, usually from 6 AM to 6 PM. Bathe afterwards, often in the sea, then go for darshan.

Free at HR&CE temples. Take a receipt from the official counter and decline any unofficial charge.

Kavadi

A devotee carries a decorated arch (kavadi) on the shoulders to the temple, usually after days of fasting and simple living. Milk kavadi (paal kavadi) carries pots of milk for abhishekam. Kavadi crowds peak at Kanda Sashti, Thaipoosam and Panguni Uthiram.

No ticket for carrying kavadi. Ask at the information centre about the kavadi mandapam.

Archanai

The priest chants the Lord's names on your behalf, with your name, birth star (nakshatram) and family name (gotram), and offers flowers and coconut. You can ask for the archanai in Tamil.

A small ticket fee at the counter. Shanmuga Archanai is the elaborate version (see the table above).

Sea bath

Bathing in the sea before darshan is a tradition here, said to wash away sins. Many also bathe with water from Nazhi Kinaru, the small freshwater well near the shore.

Free. Bring a change of clothes, and mind the waves.

Facilities and accessibility

Easy for elderly visitors

Thiruchendur is the only abode with no hill to climb. The temple is on flat ground, with a few steps down to the sanctum. The hard parts are the long standing queues and walking barefoot on hot stone, so go early in the morning or in the evening.

Where to stay and eat

Temple (Devasthanam) rooms

The temple runs several lodges and cottages close by, including Arumuga Vilas, Jeyanthinathar Viduthi, Senthil Andavar Viduthi and Velavan Kudil. Rooms are simple and family-sized, with low tariffs. Book through the temple's accommodation section on the HR&CE site, or call the Devasthanam information centre on 04639 242271. Book early for festivals and weekends. Rooms at the temple's Kumaravidangan Lodge can also be booked online on the official temple site.

Private hotels

Many lodges and hotels lie within a 5–20 minute walk of the temple and near the railway station, from budget rooms to AC family rooms.

Food

The temple serves free meals (annadhanam) to devotees at the Annadhanam mandapam. The streets leading to the temple are lined with pure-vegetarian "bhavan" restaurants serving South Indian meals and tiffin.

Before you go

  • Dress traditionally. Cover shoulders and knees. Veshti or dhoti for men and saree or churidar for women is the safe choice. Men may be asked to remove their shirts in some areas.
  • Leave phones and cameras behind. They are not allowed inside. Use the cloakroom and footwear counters at the entrance.
  • Bathe in the sea first, if you wish. Many pilgrims take a dip before darshan. The waves can be strong, so stay where others are bathing and keep children close.
  • Book paid darshan or poojas online. Special darshan, abhishekam and other sevas can be booked through the HR&CE portal, or at the temple counter.
  • Go on a weekday morning for the shortest queues. October to March has the most comfortable weather.

How to reach

Directions in Google Maps

By train

Tiruchendur has its own station (code TCN), about 1 km from the temple. A daily direct express runs from Chennai via Madurai and Tirunelveli. Tirunelveli is the nearest major junction.

By air

Thoothukudi (TCR) is closest, about 30–40 km, with flights from Chennai and Bengaluru. Madurai (IXM) has more flights and is about 3–4 hours by road. The nearest international airport is Thiruvananthapuram.

By road

Frequent buses run from Tirunelveli (about 55 km) and Thoothukudi (about 40 km). From Madurai it is roughly 180 km by car.

Festivals at Thiruchendur

Kanda SashtiAippasi (Oct–Nov). Six days, ending with Soorasamharam on the beach. The biggest event of the year.
Masi festivalMasi (Feb–Mar). A 12-day festival with processions.
Avani festivalAvani (Aug–Sep). The other major Brahmotsavam.
Vaikasi VisakamVaikasi (May–Jun). Murugan's birth star.
MonthlyKrithigai, Visakam and Sashti days bring special processions and bigger crowds.

Nearby

  • Kulasekarapattinam, about 20 km. The Mutharamman temple is famous for its Dasara festival.
  • Manapad. A quiet coastal village with a historic church on the headland.
  • Thoothukudi, about 40 km. The port city, and the nearest airport.
